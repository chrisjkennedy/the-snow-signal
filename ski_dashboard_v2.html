North America

Sierra Nevada / California
Mammoth, Palisades Tahoe, Big Bear
Bullish
Snow vs avg
+15–25%

El Niño's subtropical jet targets California's coast with unusual persistence. High-elevation Mammoth (11,053 ft summit) consistently outperforms — it sits above the rain-snow phase-change zone, a critical distinction when the airmass is warmer than normal.

⚠Snow level is the key variable. A storm delivering 2 ft of water may produce rain at 7,000 ft and powder above 9,000 ft. Always check the forecast freezing level.
Southwest / Southern Rockies
Taos, Wolf Creek, Telluride, San Juans
Bullish
Snow vs avg
+10–20%

The southern jet corridor rewards this region most consistently in El Niño years. High base elevations mean the warmer airmass still falls as snow. Taos is one of the very few major destinations where El Niño is a documented, reliable positive signal.

⚠A Super El Niño (~25% probability) amplifies this signal materially — Wolf Creek and the San Juans could see exceptional seasons.
Utah / Central Colorado
Alta, Snowbird, Park City, Breckenridge, Vail
Mixed
Snow vs avg
Near average

ENSO has minimal predictive power here — the correlation is statistically negligible across 50+ seasons. Utah hit record-low snowpack in 2025-26; year-over-year improvement is likely regardless. Do not credit El Niño for it.

⚠The popular narrative that El Niño reliably helps the Rockies is largely the 1982-83 halo effect. The data does not support it for Alta or the I-70 corridor.
Pacific Northwest / Northern Rockies
Jackson Hole, Big Sky, Mt. Bachelor, Stevens Pass
Bearish
Snow vs avg
~85% of avg

The most consistently disfavored North American region. El Niño deflects Pacific systems south, cutting off the storm parade that defines these mountains in La Niña years. Jackson Hole averaged ~85% of normal snowfall across the six strongest El Niño winters on record.

⚠Grand Targhee and Big Sky show less ENSO sensitivity due to microclimatic factors. High-elevation terrain can still deliver good individual cycles.
Northeast United States
Killington, Stowe, Jay Peak, Cannon
Bearish
Snow vs avg
~70–75% of avg

El Niño suppresses Arctic cold intrusions, compressing both the front and back of the snow window. The 2015-16 Super El Niño drove snowfall 30–40% below average at major New England resorts. The structural setup is consistently unfavorable.

⚠Negative NAO events can still produce intense Nor'easters. Book deposits, not airfare. Watch the CPC NAO index in December–January.
Canadian Rockies / BC
Whistler, Revelstoke, Banff, Big White
Bearish
Snow vs avg
~80–85% of avg

La Niña's top beneficiary becomes El Niño's clearest loser in North America. BC interior — Revelstoke, Big White — is almost entirely Pacific storm-track dependent. Coastal Whistler has some insulation from sheer elevation and orographic lift, but the directional signal is unambiguous.

⚠A Super El Niño amplifies this signal materially. If the tail scenario confirms by August, avoid non-refundable BC interior bookings.

Global Destinations

European Alps
Zermatt, Val d'Isère, St. Anton, Chamonix, Verbier
Mixed — elevation dependent
ENSO signal
Weak / noisy

Historical data shows little consistent ENSO-snowfall correlation in the Alps — the NAO is the dominant driver. El Niño's main risk is indirect: warmer background conditions raising the rain-snow line at lower resorts. Above 2,500m the signal is negligible. The 2015-16 Super El Niño produced poor lower-resort conditions and excellent glacier skiing.

⚠Book high. A Sudden Stratospheric Warming event mid-winter can override El Niño for 4–6 weeks — SSW probability rises with low Arctic sea ice extent.
Japan — Hokkaido / Tohoku
Niseko, Furano, Myoko, Nozawa Onsen
Mixed — counterintuitive
ENSO signal
Nuanced

Japan defies the North American intuition. Niseko showed above-average snowfall in 7 of 12 El Niño cycles since 1954. Japan's powder is driven by Siberian cold air crossing the Sea of Japan — a mechanism only weakly coupled to ENSO. Hakuba (Nagano) is the notable exception: below average in ~83% of El Niño years.

⚠For Japan-focused skiers: Niseko holds up historically. Hakuba is a meaningful risk in this ENSO regime.
Andes — Chile / Argentina
Portillo, Valle Nevado, Bariloche, Las Leñas
Strongly Bullish
Snow signal
Strong positive

The clearest El Niño beneficiary globally. The high central Andes respond to El Niño more directly than almost any other ski region on earth. In strong events, multi-day storm sequences have deposited extraordinary totals. Portillo (2,880m base) is the highest-conviction destination of this ENSO cycle. Season: June–September; peak window July–mid-August.

⚠A Super El Niño is essentially a structural long for Portillo. Book early — El Niño years generate unusual Northern Hemisphere interest in Andean destinations.
Australia
Perisher, Falls Creek, Thredbo
Bearish
Snow signal
Historically poor

El Niño's clearest victim globally. Warmer temperatures, reduced precipitation, early closures, and compressed seasons characterize strong El Niño winters in Australia. The 2023-24 El Niño produced record warm winter temperatures. Marginal base elevations (Perisher peaks ~2,034m) offer no buffer against warm-air intrusions.

⚠Highest-conviction avoid of this cycle if the event achieves moderate-to-strong intensity. Season runs June–September — full overlap with the strongest Southern Hemisphere El Niño signal.
Key Wildcards — Apply to All Regions

What can override the seasonal signal

El Niño Intensity
Moderate (+1.0°C) vs. Super (+2.0°C) is a dramatically different scenario. All signals amplify materially in the tail. Wait for July–August data before high-confidence booking commitments.
Sudden Stratospheric Warming
SSW events disrupt the polar vortex and send cold air south for 4–6 week windows, overriding El Niño's suppression of Arctic intrusions. Near-record-low Arctic sea ice extent raises SSW probability.
Snow Level vs. Snowfall
El Niño brings moisture but also warmer airmasses. A storm delivering 3 feet of water at a warm snow level produces rain at 7,000 ft and powder above 9,000 ft. Elevation of the freezing level matters as much as total precipitation.
Background Warming Trend
Climate change compresses the effective snow window at lower elevations in all ENSO phases. High-altitude resorts are increasingly the only reliable option — a structural shift that operates independently of any ENSO cycle.
